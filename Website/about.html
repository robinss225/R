Biography & Background

About Me

Geology student, field researcher, and lifelong student of the Himalayan landscape.

🧑‍🔬 Add your photo

Rabindra Karki · Field Work, Nepal

I am a geology student at Tribhuvan University driven by a deep curiosity about the forces that have shaped — and continue to shape — the Himalayan landscape.

Growing up in Nepal means living alongside geology in its most dramatic form: landslides that sever communities, earthquakes that reshape valleys, glaciers that feed rivers. For me, studying geology is not merely an academic pursuit — it is a responsibility to the land and people I come from.

My work focuses on the intersection of structural geology, applied geophysics, and natural hazard assessment. I believe that rigorous field investigation, combined with modern geophysical tools like Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT), provides the most reliable foundation for understanding slope instability, fault behaviour, and subsurface geology in high-mountain terrain.

Outside of fieldwork and research, I am interested in communicating geological knowledge to communities living in hazard-prone areas, and in developing accessible methodologies that can be applied across Nepal's diverse geological regions — from the Terai plains to the high Himalayan crystallines.

Philosophy

How I approach research

01

Field-First Thinking

Every geological interpretation begins in the field. I believe primary data collected through careful observation and measurement is the most reliable foundation for any research conclusion.

02

Integrated Methods

Combining classical geological mapping with modern geophysical tools — ERT, seismic refraction, remote sensing — produces a more complete picture of subsurface geology than any single method alone.

03

Community Relevance

Research that cannot be translated into practical hazard mitigation or community resilience is incomplete. I strive to ensure my findings have real-world application for the people most affected by Nepal's geological hazards.
